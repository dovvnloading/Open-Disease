# Open-Disease charter

**November 2026 | independent, agent-first computational research initiative**

## Purpose and realistic resources

Open-Disease provides a public workbench for AI agents to apply bounded research effort to difficult disease problems. The operator chooses priorities; agents investigate source material, develop reproducible analyses, test hypotheses and record failures. We are **not** a funded research group, scientific institution, clinical provider or scientifically validated discovery program. No grant or formal partnership is assumed. The default plan does **not** recruit experts or contact people in the field.

**Deliverable:** transparent source-backed research notes, reproducibility code, null or contradictory findings, and *preliminary* computational hypotheses. **Not deliverable:** promised cures, medical advice, diagnostic decisions, unverified drug targets or simulated results disguised as real evidence.

## Scope

Open-Disease operates entirely computationally. Its research includes analyses of previously collected public data, independent computational replication and mechanistic hypothesis generation. The initiative does not perform wet-lab studies, collect specimens or recruit participants. Endometriosis is the active program. Future candidates—ALS, type 1 diabetes, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and ME/CFS—are a backlog of different questions, not one general disease model. Each new program must define its own data, biological unit, validation checks and limitations.

## Operating rules

- Prefer falsifiable, finite tasks in `research/tasks/` with explicit compute budgets and a record of what actually ran.
- No source or experiment is more credible because an AI generated it. Cite primary references, protect data rights, preserve confounders and null results, and distinguish donors from cells or technical samples.
- Public-data computational exploration may proceed without outside collaborators or formal sign-off **if qualified as exploratory**. Independent replication and expert review are valuable evidence, not prerequisites for agents to start.
- Respect restrictions on human-data access and medical interpretation. Agents must not independently pursue outreach. Escalate only a specific compelling need and obtain operator approval.
- Methods and conclusions should survive another agent reproducing or falsifying them. Scientific status advances by evidence, not by number of commits, simulations or model parameters.
