# Open-Disease Research Charter

Version 0.1 — 12 November 2026

## Purpose

Accelerate credible preclinical disease research through open methods and evidence tracking. Do not represent software results as cures. A successful first milestone is a replicable computational finding, a rigorously falsified hypothesis, or a transparent resource that qualified scientists can use.

## Active program: Endometriosis

Research question: Which genes and pathways show convergent human genetic and cell-type-specific evidence, beyond cohort or menstrual-cycle confounding, and are plausible to investigate further without disregarding fertility or healthy tissue?

Output: provenance-rich candidate lists, independent replication audits and negative controls. Not output: a diagnostic classifier for patients, a therapy prescription or an unsupported causal finding.

Rationale: The 2026 eutopic-endometrium atlas includes 60 donors (35 disease, 25 control); the 2026 GWAS includes more than 100,000 cases; a menstrual-fluid pilot has only ten donors. These studies motivate investigation, but do not establish a cure. Details in DATA_SOURCES.md.

## Research principles

1. Donors, not cells, are the biological units for patient inference.
2. Distinct papers or independent patient cohorts, not random cell splits, define external validation.
3. Tissue specificity, menstrual-cycle phase, treatment, donor age, ancestry, processing batch and disease stage are potential confounders—not footnotes.
4. Genomic association does not imply a nearby gene is causal; expression differences are not proof of a therapeutic intervention point.
5. Data-use restrictions and human-subject consent supersede repository convenience.
6. Publish uncertainty, negative results, counterevidence and known failure modes.
7. Clinical efficacy and safety require specialized laboratory and ultimately regulated clinical evidence.

Governance: preserve an auditable analysis history. A clinician-scientist, biostatistician and patient representative should review any human-data interpretation before promotion as a scientific finding.
