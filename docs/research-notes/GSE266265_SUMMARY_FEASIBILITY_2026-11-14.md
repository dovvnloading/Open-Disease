# Public cell-type summaries in the Swiss endometrial atlas

**Metadata and file-schema assessment, November 14, 2026.** Duempelmann et al. provide a 2.13 MB workbook of selected, cell-type-specific differential-expression results. It can support source-conditional annotation of a reported hit. Its sheets lack the complete tested-gene denominator, donor-level counts and covariance needed for an independent rerun or a new pathway score. Absence from the workbook cannot count as failed replication. We examined source descriptions, workbook structure, header labels and archive inventories. No candidate statistics, individual cell measurements or participant records were inspected. [Primary study and supplementary data](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73020-4)

## Study and release

The full Swiss atlas comprises 35 women with endometriosis and 25 comparators. Biopsies were obtained before surgery. All participants underwent laparoscopic inspection, and histopathology confirmed disease for cases. Donors had various surgical indications, including pelvic pain, infertility and other gynecological disorders. The study excluded donors using exogenous hormones in the previous three months. Controls with adenomyosis were excluded. Its strict proliferative-phase differential-expression analysis used **12 cases and 11 controls**. Muscat fit cell-level models with capture batch as a covariate. The published contrasts included mild, severe and all endometriosis versus controls. Some small cell types were combined. The report specifies local adjusted p < 0.05, average expression > 18.53202 and exclusion of ribosomal genes. [Recruitment and statistical methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13385355/)

## Larger public bulk comparison

Fung et al. studied endometrial expression in 229 women recruited at the Royal Women's Hospital and Melbourne IVF. Table 7 records 112 affected, 48 unaffected and five unknown at the surgery clinic, and 21 affected, 41 unaffected and two unknown by IVF self-report. The recorded disease-status totals are therefore 133, 89 and seven. The paper reports cycle-adjusted disease analyses and no gene meeting its genome-wide adjusted threshold. Its supplementary workbook exposes an outcome-independent denominator. Sheet S20 has exactly 15,262 data-row positions after two header rows, matching the paper's 15,262 probes assigned to 12,321 high-detection genes. Headers include probe and gene identifiers, logFC, moderated t, raw and adjusted p-values. S21 covers 9,626 other probes whose outcome is detection versus nondetection. These distinct endpoints require separate interpretation. [2018 study and Table 7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-29462-y) · [Versioned 4,476,677-byte supplementary workbook](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6065421.1/41598_2018_29462_MOESM2_ESM.xlsx)

This is a public, aggregate-only route to a separate gene-level check, conditional on prespecifying probe mapping and verifying the case–control contrast orientation for directional inference. The workbook SHA-256 is `b83a3d32a5882f750d978f4de1a6473fdd5d7627dbccf7d7c1774162e104e8e3`. Its marginal probe results cannot reconstruct donor-level within-array ranks, gene–gene covariance, or the exact pathway inference used in Finland and Rome. Any public recheck must account for all nominated targets, including unmeasured ones.

Mortlock et al. profiled 206 women (143 recorded cases, 63 controls) by RNA sequencing and likewise found no genome-wide significant uterine expression differences after correction. They recruited from the same two Melbourne clinics. Their paper links an eQTL resource; eQTL effects describe variation with genotype. We found no accession for the individual expression matrix or source documentation guaranteeing that no donors overlapped the 2018 microarray cohort in the cited articles. Counting the two as independent validation cohorts would require further evidence. [2020 RNA-seq study, Table I, Data Availability and Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1093/humrep/dez279)
