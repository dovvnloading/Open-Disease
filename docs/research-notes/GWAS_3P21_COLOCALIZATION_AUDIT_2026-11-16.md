# What the endometriosis association at 3p21.31 establishes

**Decision: INCONCLUSIVE.** Inherited susceptibility maps to this region. HYAL3 has a published variant-to-expression link with clinically diagnosed endometriosis. The evidence leaves open which regional transcript mediates risk, whether the mechanism operates in cycling endometrium, and whether the HYAL3 and BSN markers tag the same disease signal. The HYAL3 candidate lies outside a ±500 kb window centered on the reported BSN lead. This is a literature and metadata assessment with an offline coordinate and gene-list check. No independent disease–QTL posterior was estimated. [2023 study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-023-01323-z) · [2026 study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-026-02582-2) · [Verification output](../../evidence/gwas-3p21-colocalization-audit/verification.json)

The separation on GRCh37 is **680,549 bp**. The ±500 kb window centered on rs1352889 ends at 50,152,148. The HYAL3 candidate is 180,549 bp beyond it. Rahmioglu's European conditional analysis used 1000 Genomes Phase 3 v5 European LD and detected additional genome-wide significant signals at four other loci. Rahmioglu defined loci around leads and merged nearby windows. A later analysis explicitly identifies rs1352889 as the BSN GWAS lead, but we could not verify the original S3/S8 row or reconstruct any merged boundary. The single detected BSN-region signal therefore cannot establish that the study tested and excluded a second signal at the HYAL3 marker. Physical distance also cannot establish independence. The r²=1 in Rahmioglu Table 2 compares rs1352889 with the pain marker **rs11709734**. We have no verified pairwise LD for rs1352889 versus rs13100173. The 2023 study built European 99% credible sets under one causal variant per conditional signal, uniform variant priors and prior variance 0.04 on the log-OR scale. The paper explicitly places rs1352889 in a credible set. None of its six variants with posterior >0.5 lies at BSN. The set size, complete membership and individual probabilities in S10–S12 were unavailable for direct verification. Koller used ancestry-specific UK Biobank LD for COJO (S5). Their PAINTOR analysis used Gibbs sampling over ±1 Mb from each lead and allowed multiple effects, incorporating selected functional annotations. Its disease-only posteriors (S7), COJO conditional associations and the expression-colocalization posterior (S8) answer different questions. No locus-specific S5 or S7 row or complete 2026 credible set was verified. HyPrColoc v0.0.2 assumes at most one causal variant per trait in the tested region. Koller retained PPFC >0.7 and candidate-SNP contribution >0.5. Their 29 positive rows cover 15 genes and 13 variants, with one uterus row. This selected table lacks the full regional denominator, including measurements or model failures for competing genes. For uterus, its rounded results in the text are 0.82 for PPFC and 0.72 for the SNP contribution. We did not find the numerical priors, actual variant list or an invocation for the uterus comparison. The method literature considers marginal association priors such as 10⁻⁴ and conditional sharing probabilities such as 0.01–0.05. Substituting a package default or reweighting 0.817 alone would not reproduce the analysis. [Koller methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13195585/) · [HyPrColoc model and sensitivity](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20885-8)

**Competing genes.** The rs1352889 endometrial eQTL annotation (Table 2, details in S31) and the four selected regional uterine TWAS genes intersect at HYAL3, RBM6 and GMPPB. UBA7, KLHDC8B, NCKIPSD and CCDC36 occur only in the first list. TMEM115 occurs only in the second. The comparisons use different variants, phenotypes, tissues and selection thresholds. A comparison conditional on the same disease component has yet to establish an advantage for any transcript. [2023 Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10042257/#T2) · [Computed set comparison](../../evidence/gwas-3p21-colocalization-audit/verification.json)

**Competing mechanisms.** The same rs13100173–HYAL3 pairing passed the authors' criteria in vagina, ovary, mammary tissue and both sigmoid and transverse colon. All six tests reuse the same clinical GWAS, and GTEx samples multiple tissues from some donors. The variant also changes HYAL3 residue 113 (His/Tyr). On the minus-strand CCDS2815.1 coding sequence, the first base of codon 113 maps 336 bases below 50,295,602, giving the GRCh38 coordinate above. A coding effect remains plausible even if the expression and disease signals share this exact allele. A separate metabolomics study also associated rs13100173 with plasma N-acetylgalactosamine-4-sulfate. Neither result demonstrates an endometrial biochemical mechanism. ## Tissue and independence

GTEx requested uterine corpus including endometrium along one edge and underlying myometrium. Pathologists found the lining often too autolyzed to evaluate. Viable endometrium was usually inactive basalis. GTEx v8 lists 129 uterus donors for cis-eQTL mapping. Koller quotes 153 uterus samples in the TWAS tissue description. Neither figure establishes how many informative, cycling endometrial donors contributed to HYAL3 expression. Reprocessing the same uterus data in the eQTL Catalogue can test pipeline sensitivity. It cannot localize the signal to endometrium or supply a second donor cohort. [GTEx harvesting instructions, §4.3.6.27](https://biospecimens.cancer.gov/resources/sops/docs/GTEx_SOPs/BBRB-PR-0004-W1%20GTEx%20Tissue%20Harvesting%20Work%20Instruction.pdf) · [GTEx histopathology](https://doi.org/10.5858/arpa.2023-0468-OA) · [GTEx v8](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaz1776) · [Koller TWAS methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13195585/)

Independent of GTEx, the Australian 206-woman RNA-seq study sampled reproductive-age, cycle-staged endometrium. A UK RNA-seq cohort of 163 contributed regional endometrial eQTLs in the 2023 study. Its Results give a pooled N of 368, while Methods give 206+163=369. The paper gives no third independent cohort corresponding to this discrepancy. Fung's 229-woman array and Mortlock's RNA-seq series derive from Melbourne recruitment, with some material assayed on both platforms in the larger program. Exact overlap of these two analytic subsets is unverified. These resources support local gene regulation in endometrium, but no verified independent, conditional 3p21.31 comparison separated the genes. [Mortlock et al.](https://doi.org/10.1093/humrep/dez279) · [Fung et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-29462-y) · [Cohort and technologies](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-41979-z) · [Rahmioglu, Methods and S34–35](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10042257/)

The 2023 endometrial SMR analysis deliberately removed Oxford and Melbourne from its disease GWAS. Substituting the public no-23andMe GWAS would change both the disease input and the overlap check. Koller combined includes the earlier IEGC data with UKB, while the clinical analysis uses UKB and other biobanks directly. The clinical analysis does not require 23andMe self-report. The same clinical biobanks also contribute to the combined analysis. Exact cross-study donor intersections are unknown. Overlapping genetic inputs rule out treating the papers, two definitions, or different GTEx tissue rows as independent replications. [Rahmioglu, Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10042257/) · [Koller, Fig. 1 and Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13195585/)

## Execution and feasibility

The prior project had already extracted the S8/S13 findings, tested a 23-gene genetic panel against cohort expression and background genes, tested RBM6 direction and signed three- and seven-gene scores, compared 125 nominated genes with the published genetic and blood-group resources, and checked five endometrial SMR genes across tissue contrasts. The primary three-gene score moved −0.599 percentile points in Finland and +2.804 in Mumbai and failed its fixed criterion. Neither this failure nor a positive case–control result determines what a germline allele does. The prior India GWAS portability audit addressed a different marker family. We did not rerun these analyses. ## One experiment that would change the decision

**Run one conditional, multi-gene colocalization across both markers in cycle-staged endometrium, using histologically identified myometrium as a tissue comparator.** Obtain unthresholded regional summary statistics from the European clinical GWAS and compartment-specific molecular QTLs, with each contributor's effective N, allele coding, variant coverage, signed LD and statement of cohort overlap. First check whether the UK 163-donor endometrial release covers both markers, all assayed cis genes and histologically assigned cycle stages. Use an overlap-excluded European clinical GWAS if needed and label the changed input. Reuse of GTEx would address the original association only. If a suitable myometrial comparison is missing, tissue specificity remains untested.

Use the [fixed interval and allele rules](../../evidence/gwas-3p21-colocalization-audit/design.md), cover both peaks, and report per-gene coverage and strength of each eQTL before interpreting differences. Include HYAL3, RBM6, TMEM115, GMPPB and all other adequately measured regional genes. Pair each disease component with each molecular component, retain distinct-signal evidence, and compare single-signal priors with multiple-signal fits. Apply the same rules to the controls. A component that repeatedly shares with one well-measured gene in endometrium after accounting for neighboring signals would justify further study of that gene and compartment. Sharing across several genes or compartments, disappearance after conditioning, or inadequate power to distinguish them would preserve the inconclusive decision. Even a positive result would leave mediation versus a coding effect open. [Signal-level colocalization method](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1009440)
