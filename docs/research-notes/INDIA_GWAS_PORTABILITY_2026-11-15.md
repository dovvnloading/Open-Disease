# How well do 21 Indian endometriosis risk variants transfer across populations?

**Exploratory human-data computation, November 15, 2026.** Anand and colleagues reported 21 suggestive lead variants from an Indian endometriosis study. We checked the entire published set against the earlier public Rahmioglu GWAS and a Central/South Asian stratum of UK Biobank. Neither comparison recovered a variant in the Indian risk direction after correction across the 21 tests. In the larger, predominantly European comparison, 12 of the 15 assessable markers had simultaneous 95% intervals entirely inside odds ratios 0.909 to 1.10. This bounds the size of their average associations in that study. South Asian and stage-specific effects remain unresolved. [Indian discovery](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-69642-9) · [External meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-023-01323-z) · [Pan-UK Biobank](https://pan.ukbb.broadinstitute.org/downloads/)

## The IL37 lead

The Indian study assigned rs7563305 to the IL37 region. The repository previously nominated an interleukin-37 pathway score from gene expression, which varied in direction across cohorts. We noticed the common label before selecting this comparison. IL37 (Entrez 27178) was one of the 19 frozen Finnish pathway members. It was the sole member missing from the Swiss expression projection. Restricting Finland and Rome to the other 18 retained the opposite signs (+1.561 and −2.599 percentile points). That earlier reversal can therefore be observed without measuring IL37 itself. The variant literature supplies a nearby-gene annotation and does not establish an IL37 regulatory effect. [Frozen membership](../../evidence/cross-cohort-2026-11-14/nominations.json) · [Swiss projection](../../evidence/external-bulk-2026-11-14/locked_hgnc_projection.tsv) · [18-member sensitivity](../../evidence/swiss-glandular-2026-11-14/prior-cohorts.json)

| Study | Population and phenotype | Odds ratio per C allele (marginal 95% CI) | Two-sided p |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Indian discovery | 1,288 cases, 1,235 controls. Of 1,279 staged cases, 1,094 had stage III/IV disease. | 1.488 (1.250–1.772) | 0.00000874 |
| Independent external meta-analysis, public non-23andMe release | 470,866 European and 3,294 Japanese participants according to release metadata, with mixed case ascertainment | 1.026 (0.999–1.054) | 0.06067 |
| Pan-UK Biobank sensitivity | 94 Central/South Asian female cases, 3,892 controls, hospital-code-based phecode 615 | 0.626 (0.419–0.935) | 0.02203 |

We inverted the published Indian effect for the T allele and used its rounded log-odds standard error of 0.089 to calculate the interval. The large external study’s familywise interval was 0.984–1.070. The Central/South Asian estimate pointed in the opposite direction, with adjusted p = 0.463. Its small case count and ancestry/trait-level heritability QC flag limit inference. The results weaken the hypothesis of a broadly shared 1.49-fold effect at this marker. They leave open smaller effects, different patterns of linked alleles, clinical subtype differences, and selection-related inflation of the discovery estimate.

The one-sided sign test for excess agreement in the primary analysis gave p = 0.696. It assumes independent fair signs across distinct discovered loci under the null. All 15 of the primary simultaneous upper limits fell below the corresponding Indian point estimate. Those Indian estimates were selected genome-wide for large associations and carry sampling uncertainty. The upper-limit comparison uses them descriptively. The 10% odds threshold is a sensitivity benchmark with no established clinical meaning. [Primary variant table](../../evidence/india-gwas-portability-2026-11-15/variants.tsv) · [Complete Pan-UKB table](../../evidence/india-gwas-portability-2026-11-15/panukb.tsv) · [Counts and controls](../../evidence/india-gwas-portability-2026-11-15/summary.json)

The smallest primary p was at rs12631821, with risk-allele OR 1.030 (95% CI 1.001–1.059, Bonferroni p = 0.821). Six tags lacked an exact match. In a second scan by chromosome and coordinate, none of those six had a row at the published GRCh38 position. Two previously established controls chosen from Anand Supplementary Table S5, WNT4-region rs10917151-A and CDKN2B-AS1-region rs10122243-T, gave OR 1.162 (p = 3.08 × 10⁻²⁵) and 1.091 (p = 1.37 × 10⁻¹⁴). The validation study contributed to the original identification of these controls. Their recovery checks extraction and allele orientation.

The most significant Indian tag, rs35236165 near SHISA2, was absent from the primary file. In the Central/South Asian file its unsigned p was 0.526. Its A/T allele pair and near-balanced frequency prevented a signed estimate under our rules. In a **post-primary sensitivity** we searched all named nonlead variants in Supplementary Table S2 within 250 kb of all six absent leads. Eleven qualified, seven were present, six were directionally resolvable, and none achieved nominal significance (minimum p = 0.2333). At rs9512061, 166 bp from rs35236165, India reported OR 1.336 for C. The primary external estimate was 0.992 (0.970–1.015; p = 0.4812). This is a separate nearby observation. Neither physical distance nor presence in the Indian discovery table establishes equal linkage disequilibrium in the external population. ## Design and sources

The 21-variant family comes from Anand et al. Table 2. We obtained both alleles, frequency, log-odds standard error and p from their Supplementary Table S2, checked risk-allele frequency against S4, and matched the GRCh38 position from S2 in the primary GWAS. We kept the original 21 denominator throughout. For aligned rows we report source two-sided p, 95% Wald OR intervals using 1.96 × SE, and simultaneous intervals using the 1 − 0.05/(2 × 21) normal quantile. Palindromic alleles required consistent risk frequencies away from 0.5 as documented in the analysis plan. The full panel, matched coordinates, per-variant test criteria, moment of each sensitivity decision and exclusions are available for review. The local analysis plan preceded inspection of external candidate effects and was not publicly preregistered. [Analysis plan](../../evidence/india-gwas-portability-2026-11-15/analysis-plan.md) · [Publisher’s supplement](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-026-69642-9/MediaObjects/41598_2026_69642_MOESM2_ESM.pdf)

GCST90205183 is the Rahmioglu public analysis excluding 23andMe, covering 474,160 participants according to harmonised release metadata. Its 8,312,193 harmonised rows differ from the 8,593,419 before harmonisation. The earlier consortium population was of European and Japanese ancestry, distinct from the Indian-only ECGRI discovery comparison. The Indian-plus-Japanese meta-analysis was not used to select or estimate the 21 discovery effects. The external meta-analysis combines different diagnostic definitions and stages. [Release and checksums](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/gwas/summary_statistics/GCST90205001-GCST90206000/GCST90205183/harmonised/) · [GWAS Catalog terms](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/docs/faq/)

For the UKB sensitivity we used the female phecode 615 stratum, the GRCh37 positions from Indian Table 2, and the exact ref/alt allele pair. Pan-UKB codes ALT as the effect allele and releases the negative base-10 log of the two-sided p. One variant had low confidence and one was strand-ambiguous. For A/T and C/G, we required the discovery risk frequency to be at least 0.10 from 0.5 and the aligned UKB control frequency to be within 0.10 of the discovery frequency. Cohort-level heritability QC was flagged as `h2_z_insignificant`. Ancestry designations and surgical case mix differ from ECGRI. UK Biobank also contributed to the Rahmioglu consortium. We present the two validation analyses separately and make no claim that their participants are disjoint. [Phenotype and variant definitions](https://pan.ukbb.broadinstitute.org/docs/per-phenotype-files/) · [Pan-UKB and UK Biobank attribution](https://pan.ukbb.broadinstitute.org/downloads/)

## Reproduce

Inputs were public aggregate statistics. All execution used the standard Python library and Poppler. The publisher’s CC BY-NC-ND source supplement is available from the publisher. Original figures, supplement, raw GWAS and participant-level records are excluded from this contribution. A local run on Python 3.12.14, pdftotext 25.03.0, and base repository commit `7a566619d0c67f534ecb3a49d7d84656608764bd` took 31 seconds for the primary scan. No random sampling was used.

Place the publisher’s supplement and the GWAS Catalog harmonised gzip in `data/raw/` as named below. The primary script checks SHA-256 `f3b072f47d0a6ebd8588fdc015b4d56bf64c817b6cbfb320a999663156431469` for the 1,326,088-byte PDF and the catalog MD5 `b5a0ce654a6e738b056048aee4296a4a` for the 165,797,641-byte gzip (independent SHA-256 `83aae9a1ac888fdbbddd12bdbf9caec01962fbbf95327309d0e56da28583e8ce`). The Pan-UKB sensitivity can fetch the 21 pinned compressed windows plus the header, totaling 1,837,280 bytes. The windows were located using the publisher's tabix index (SHA-256 `eb88f925b26ce99402e5f698634fc74e6ade44f448d85597369cfda110ace091`), with manifest SHA-256 `58343c50b06be5cee7a56a1d1a38c30065aa4321a8d8e6b3471d971e0a5b812c`. All range hashes and GRCh37 positions are supplied.
