# What clinical evidence accompanies the Erlangen peritoneal arrays?

**Literature and metadata assessment, November 16, 2026.** E-MTAB-694 links within-person specimens of tissue classified as a lesion and tissue classified as peritoneum in 13 people with endometriosis. An accession-specific clinical table supplies menstrual phase for all 13 under the published patient-number convention. A positive microscopic diagnosis in one sampled site and a negative diagnosis in another, at comparable pelvic subsites, remain unverified for these 13 pairs. The study enrolled zero noncase peritoneal donors. [Sohler et al., primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/1933719112451147) · [E-MTAB-694](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/arrayexpress/studies/E-MTAB-694) · [Earlier donor census](../../evidence/peritoneal-control-audit/sohler.json)

## Which records were recovered?

Sohler et al. describe pathology classification of every lesion and histological review of case peritoneum and uterine tissue. Their article locates one deeply infiltrating lesion in the peritoneal wall and reports invasion greater than 4 mm in that example. It gives no uniform superficial-lesion criterion for the array series. The microscopy description of stroma next to a lesion concerns tissue within a lesion section. The array manifest names the separately assayed tissue simply as peritoneum. Neither source establishes the subsite of that peritoneal biopsy, its distance from a lesion or an explicit microscopic absence of endometriosis in the assayed material or its paired section. [Author institution’s full text, Methods and Discussion](https://open.fau.de/handle/openfau/4175) · [Submitter’s sample manifest](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/fire/E-MTAB-/694/E-MTAB-694/Files/E-MTAB-694.sdrf.txt)

The primary paper refers to supplementary Table 1 for lesion sites and classification. The author institution's public record contains one original 19-page article PDF (1,085,701 bytes, SHA-256 `64141c32a69264f8c7bb7ab18e3552b6f8049ec75f5f6777a15de01698698ab8`). Its single embedded file is a PDF production settings file. We found no original clinical supplement in the deposited article, the Springer landing page or the indexed Figshare/DataCite records for the article DOI. The legacy SAGE supplement endpoint returned HTTP 403. This leaves the detailed site annotations described by the authors **unrecovered**. [FAU original-file listing](https://open.fau.de/server/api/core/bundles/d5f10058-197d-4dcb-a014-ad9f23f35762/bitstreams) · [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1177/1933719112451147) · [Figshare resource DOI lookup](https://api.figshare.com/v2/articles?resource_doi=10.1177%2F1933719112451147) · [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/1933719112451147)

Jiang et al. published a secondary clinical table that explicitly names E-MTAB-694 and credits the original article. Its six populated data columns are patient ID, age, revised American Fertility Society stage, menstrual phase, lesion availability and uterine tissue availability. The only other sheet is empty. The table covers the 19 donors with lesion or uterine tissue, including all 13 lesion–peritoneum pairs. It omits the eight case donors who contributed only peritoneum and contains no anatomical site, depth, negative histology, specimen number, sampling distance or batch field. The clinical table is a covariate source for the original cohort. Counting its rows as additional recruitment would duplicate observations. [Jiang et al., Table S1 and caption](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.8730/supp-1) · [Open-access study, provenance of S1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7060988/#supplementary-material)

**Linkage qualification.** Jiang calls its key the E-MTAB-694 patient ID and uses integers. The original article identifies patients by number and the SDRF supplies both an individual characteristic and an experimental factor with the value `patient N`. The audit used only this stated study-local number and checked both SDRF fields. All 19 keys exist uniquely at donor level, cover exactly the 19 with lesion or uterine tissue and agree with all 38 independent yes/no tissue-availability fields in the manifest. Ages, phases, row order and sample characteristics played no part in assignment. Neither the secondary table nor the retrieved primary files supply a separate two-column key asserting the equivalence. Phase attribution therefore relies on that explicit formatting interpretation. The original phase criteria also used uterine molecular and histological evidence alongside surgical serum hormones and menstrual history. The paired phases should be reported as secondary annotations. ## Can other pathology studies fill the missing records?

| Strehl et al., Erlangen | Histology of 65 lesions in 47 women, with operative documentation of lesion location and color. | The study measured morphology and immunohistochemistry. | Khan et al., Nagasaki | Immunohistological examination of visually normal tissue detected occult lesions in 23/151 donors with visible disease and 4/62 without visible disease. | These observations explain why a laparoscopic label and biopsy pathology answer different questions. The sampled sites and recruitment differ from the Finnish and Erlangen studies. This study supplies no RNA or correction factor for either. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1093/humrep/det438) |

**Experimental implication.** The [physical scan audit](PERITONEAL_CONTROL_FOLLOWUP_2026-11-16.md#why-the-thirteen-german-pairs-cannot-adjudicate-lesion-specificity) found complete separation by instrument and acquisition period, blocking estimation of a separate tissue effect. Menstrual phase can characterize its donor mix. The verified metadata cannot estimate an effect specific to superficial, histologically positive lesions above histologically negative same-site tissue. That would require a public pathology-to-specimen link documenting the peritoneal biopsy findings and a sampling subsite for both arms. A distance measure or specified distant collection rule would further distinguish a local response. A comparison against noncase peritoneum additionally requires that third arm in a design with comparable collection and assay conditions.
