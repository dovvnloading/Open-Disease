# The Finnish uterine expression contrast reverses broadly in Rome

**November 15, 2026 · Exploratory human-data computation.** We applied the entire Finnish endometriosis expression contrast to separately recruited proliferative uterine biopsies in Rome. Every one of eight Rome cases scored below every one of six controls in the direction defined by Finland. Native array timestamps reveal a critical design limitation. Within each of the two scan dates containing both groups, the scanner processed every control before every case. The seven-biopsy Mumbai cohort showed little aggregate alignment with either reference. [Aggregate results and measured gene IDs](../../evidence/genome-transport-2026-11-15)

When each gene was given equal weight in the direction of its Finnish difference, the mean case–control change was −2.55 percentile points in Rome. Of 159 genes in the top tenth by absolute standardized change in both studies, 155 went in opposite directions. Repeating the whole-profile test after removing all 125 genes in the earlier nominations and nominated pathways, or removing the 121 strongest Finnish genes, gave the same two-sided tail count. **Physical processing order limits the interpretation.** One of three Rome scan dates had three cases and zero controls and could not supply a within-date contrast. The other two dates contained three cases/three controls and two cases/three controls. With equal weight for the two dates, the separately estimated score differences were −1.103 and −0.893, respectively, with a combined sign-aligned difference of −2.89 percentile points. Every within-date case–control pair (9 + 6) had the case scan later and the Finnish-oriented case score lower. A date block accounts for day-level differences while leaving within-date order completely segregated by disease group. Unknown order-related effects could produce this separation. The data also cannot establish that order caused the expression pattern. Conditional p-values rely on exchangeability of the case labels in the indicated groups. Case/control recruitment, other benign conditions in Rome controls, hormone exposure, tissue mixture and phase may contribute. **Technical controls also differed across the groups, with uncertainty.** After discovering the ordering issue, we specified three diagnostics before reading their values. We tested the mean ACTB/GAPDH 3′ minus 5′ probe signal, and separate averages of the four hybridization-control targets (bioB, bioC, bioD, cre) and four poly-A-control targets (dap, lys, phe, thr). For exogenous controls, each target received equal weight after averaging its corresponding `AFFX-r2` probes. The hybridization-control probes were 0.314 log2 units lower in cases within date (6/200 allocations, nominal p = 0.030, three-test Holm p = 0.090). The ACTB/GAPDH gradient was 0.560 log2 units lower (Holm p = 0.15), and the poly-A control probes were 0.158 log2 units higher (Holm p = 0.15). All three use the deposited RMA-normalized signals. The study-specific control doses and raw RNA integrity values are unknown. These measurements cannot identify what produced the broad gene reversal. **Mumbai did not discriminate between the references.** The study provided mid-proliferative uterine RNA sequencing for four cases and three controls. Each donor contributed one measurement. ## Analysis and verification

The Finnish reference is the complete previously published screen of 12,044 signed, null-standardized gene contrasts, computed across seven assay-run-by-phase strata in 12 cases and 13 controls. FOSB was excluded in that screen. The full vector had been public before this work; the earlier five-gene and five-pathway results in Rome were known. We specified the new statistic and sensitivity checks locally before calculating the genome-wide Rome contrast. The Mumbai extension followed the unrestricted Rome result. The date-blocked extension followed the scan-date census. This is an exploratory retrospective comparison, with no public prospective registration of the new hypotheses.

For each target gene we divided the target cohort's case–control difference by its label-independent randomization standard deviation, calculated as the cohort's pooled-across-donors sample SD multiplied by the square root of the sum of inverse group sizes. We then took the mean of the gene-wise products with the signed reference. Each assignment moved whole donor profiles together, preserving cross-gene correlation. The null used all assignments of eight of 14 Rome biopsies or four of seven Mumbai biopsies. The secondary Rome date test retained scales fixed from all 14 arrays and used the mean of the two within-date group contrasts. The original 12,044-gene correlation coefficients, counts, pair rankings and omissions are descriptive. The permutation unit is the donor, with no gene-independence tests. Pair rankings describe these specimens only. Across the exploratory analyses in this research program, no family-wide error rate was established.

All ten previously published Rome differences and exact tail counts reproduced. A separate implementation parsed the complete Rome probe matrix and annotation, calculated probe midranks by sorted search, and enumerated the entire permutation distribution of each gene's difference directly. It obtained the same five unrestricted scores and extreme counts in Rome and Mumbai, including the transcript-first sensitivity, and verified exact Mumbai feature membership. Mumbai's transcript mapping and fixed-point FPKMs used the existing repository loader in both implementations. A separate combination-based within-date computation agreed on all 200 conditional null values. The calculations used Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5 and SciPy 1.17.0 without random sampling. ## Reproduction

Install the repository as described in the README. The [Rome source instructions](GSE25628_FOSB_RESULTS_2026-11-13.md) identify the original checksummed matrix and annotation. The five required [Mumbai and HGNC source files](../../evidence/external-bulk-2026-11-14/mumbai-source.json) total 26,459,322 bytes. They are included in the existing `python scripts/fetch_external_bulk_sources.py --fetch` workflow. For the date analysis, save the GSE25628 GEO GSM brief metadata as `GSE25628.soft` under `data/private/matrisome-audit/` and the first 2,048 compressed bytes of each of its 22 linked native CEL files as `GSE25628/<accession>.part` beneath the same directory. The audit checks native geometry, dated headers, all file references and the combined header digest. [Rome public GEO record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE25628)

```bash
export OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1
src=evidence/cross-cohort-2026-11-14
out=results/genome-transport
rome=data/private/gse25628
mumbai=data/private/gse153739
hgnc=data/private/gse266265/hgnc_complete_set_2026-10-06.tsv
mkdir -p "$out"
python scripts/finland_rome_transport.py --evidence "$src" --rome "$rome" --out "$out/finland_rome_transport.json"
python scripts/rome_scan_transport.py --evidence "$src" --rome "$rome" --scan-audit data/private/matrisome-audit --out "$out/rome_scan_transport.json"
python scripts/rome_quality_controls.py --matrix "$rome/GSE25628_series_matrix.txt.gz" --scan-audit data/private/matrisome-audit --out "$out/rome_quality_controls.json"
python scripts/mumbai_genome_transport.py --evidence "$src" --rome "$rome" --mumbai "$mumbai" --hgnc "$hgnc" --out "$out/mumbai_genome_transport.json" --genes-out "$out/mumbai_genes.tsv"
python scripts/verify_genome_transport.py --screen "$src/discovery_gene.tsv" --rome "$rome" --rome-results "$out/finland_rome_transport.json" --mumbai "$mumbai" --hgnc "$hgnc" --mumbai-results "$out/mumbai_genome_transport.json" --genes "$out/mumbai_genes.tsv"
python -m pytest -q tests/test_genome_transport.py
```
