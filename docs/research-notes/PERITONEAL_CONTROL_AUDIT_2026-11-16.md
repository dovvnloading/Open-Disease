# The missing peritoneal control for the five-gene endometriosis score

**NO-GO for attributing the score to lesion-specific biology.** Finland’s EndometDB is the strongest public human transcriptomic cohort we found. It includes superficial lesions, peritoneum from people with endometriosis, and peritoneum from laparoscopically negative controls. It establishes the control donors’ diagnosis. Its public release does not identify which case peritoneal biopsies were microscopically negative, where the two peritoneal comparison groups were sampled, or how far the case biopsies lay from visible disease. Running the score across those three labels would leave the principal biological ambiguity in place. [Gabriel et al., Methods and Tables 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-020-00623-x) · [Native release](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.12195975.v1) · [Aggregate native audit](../../evidence/peritoneal-control-audit/endometdb.json)

**A narrower falsification is feasible.** Fourteen confirmed healthy-control donors in the native Finnish release supplied both peritoneum and uterine endometrium. Eight have both tissues in one shared assay run. A higher score in peritoneum within these women would show that the direction can occur without surgically detected endometriosis. A small or uncertain difference would leave lesion specificity unresolved. We froze this comparison for separate authorization, including exclusions for unverifiable chips and a procedure for repeated arrays. [Prospective normal-anatomy protocol](NORMAL_PERITONEUM_UTERUS_PLAN_2026-11-16.md)

**Evidence level:** literature and metadata assessment, November 16, 2026. No expression outcomes were examined or computed for this audit. The existing 23/25 external lesion–uterus pairs and six core–adjacent comparisons answer different questions. The earlier core-minus-adjacent mean was −0.46 percentile points, with a 95% common-shift interval from −6.38 to +6.40. That interval leaves biologically appreciable differences possible. [Panel synthesis](LESION_PANEL_SYNTHESIS_2026-11-15.md) · [Previous matched analysis](ADJACENT_PERITONEUM_RESULTS_2026-11-15.md) · [Previous Fonseca audit](FONSECA_PERITONEUM_COMPARATOR_2026-11-15.md)

## What would distinguish the explanations?

| Donor-level comparison | What it could establish | What remains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Superficial lesion versus histologically negative, preferably distant case peritoneum from a comparable pelvic subsite | Whether the frozen score concentrates in the lesion above the surrounding or more distant case tissue | Whole-biopsy cellular composition can account for a difference |
| Negative case peritoneum versus same-subsite peritoneum in laparoscopically negative donors | Whether the nonlesional peritoneal baseline differs by disease status | Recruitment indication, exposures and systemic or local responses remain possible |
| Noncase peritoneum versus the same donor’s uterus | Whether the direction previously seen with lesions can occur across organs in controls | Cannot apportion lesion, field and cell-composition effects in cases |

These contrasts require sample-to-donor attribution, clinical diagnosis separated from biopsy pathology, assay/collection comparability and replication across people. We gave higher priority to anatomically informative designs and to a documented tissue examination than to numbers of arrays or cells. Documented distance would discriminate local from more widespread responses. Lack of distance alone was not the basis for rejecting EndometDB.

## EndometDB by donor, specimen and assay

Cases came from four Finnish hospitals. Controls underwent tubal sterilization at Turku. Surgery and biopsy histopathology confirmed case status, and inspection at laparoscopy excluded endometriosis in controls. Tissues for RNA were snap-frozen within ten minutes. The authors staged the cycle using questionnaire, uterine histology and serum progesterone. Cases and controls differed in age on average (32 and 39 years) and in surgical indication. The study reports stage, hormonal medication and cycle categories. The native origin codes do not establish a hospital for each specimen. [Study and clinical covariates](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7455745/) · [Trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01301885)

The paper describes the full publication cohort. Native counts require agreement between recruitment category and confirmed status plus a native microarray assay. Five lesion assays and four peritoneal assays belonged to control recruits with **unexpected endometriosis** and were excluded. Replicate labels and multiple samples are retained for this census and collapsed when counting donors. Seventeen cases contributed both a lesion and case peritoneum, nine within a common run. Eight of those nine had one specimen per arm within their shared run. Nine lesion donors contributed across multiple runs. Summing run cells or tissue arms would double-count people. Runs 9–14 each contained all three peritoneal groups, although some cells contain a single donor. Run 1102 lacked control peritoneum. [Cross-tabulations, pairing and exclusions](../../evidence/peritoneal-control-audit/endometdb.json)

**Biopsy evidence.** The publication describes the tissues as histologically confirmed and provides surgical ascertainment and lesion classification. The earlier Finnish work describes parallel aliquots for pathology and macroscopically normal patient peritoneum. The original sampling account specifically discusses unresolved microscopic lesions. All 163 rows of the released specimen pathology table link to uterine tissue. The native codebook has no lesion histopathology values. The peritoneum code describes the tissue, with no assessment of glands/stroma, named pelvic subsite or distance. These facts limit verification of what was profiled. They neither contradict the pathologists’ diagnoses nor establish positive or negative histology for an unreported aliquot. [Earlier Finnish sampling methods](https://doi.org/10.1093/humrep/dey026) · [Huhtinen, sampling and discussion](https://www.utupub.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/59203/AnnalesD895Huhtinen.pdf) · [Native schema and counts](../../evidence/peritoneal-control-audit/endometdb.json)

**Assay comparison.** The GEO brief SOFT and submitters’ link workbook account for all 408 array records in 162 link groups. We excluded explicitly marked replicates and required agreement between the tissue description, tissue characteristic and reported healthy status or case stage I–IV. The GEO link groups and the native participant/run identifiers are different namespaces. Their versions and filters yield different counts. Matching them by age, phase, ordering or similar totals would invent a crosswalk. [GEO series and sample-link workbook](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE141549) · [GEO aggregate audit](../../evidence/peritoneal-control-audit/geo-platforms.json)

WG-6 supplies an address for each of the five frozen Entrez genes in its 48,701-address manifest, including TMEM255A under FAM70A and JCHAIN under IGJ. The authors processed the chip generations separately before creating a combined, batch-corrected matrix. A GEO platform declaration cannot verify the physical chip of an individual native assay. Prior physical-chip checks concerned a different uterine subset. The five-probe audit establishes technical potential, independent of any measured expression. **Medication and phase.** The native survey stores false for all 24 control-peritoneum donors. The source schema defaults to false. The paper and GEO each assign 12 of 24 control-peritoneum samples to the medication category. Among the 14 GEO WG-6 groups containing both control tissues, six pairs carry secretory labels, two proliferative, four medication and two unknown. In the native namespace, six of the eight same-run pairs have classifiable matched uterine histology (three proliferative, three secretory). The two sets of phase counts cannot identify the same individuals. Exposure timing and washout remain unverified. No native visit or operation date proves that paired tissues were collected on the same day. Within-donor pairing improves on a cross-donor treatment adjustment but cannot remove tissue-specific hormonal responses. **Independence and rights.** Gabriel explicitly links earlier Turku work to this database. García-Alonso et al. reuse the Finnish peritoneal arrays. The GEO deposit, Figshare v1 and EndometDB browser describe one recruitment program. Open-Disease also used Finnish uterine tissue in prior analyses. The lesion panel was frozen in a different ovarian cohort and its original external pairs came from other studies. This proposed endpoint is new within a previously used cohort. No cross-study identity linkage was attempted. Figshare v1 explicitly licenses the deposit under CC BY 4.0. GEO is public and cautions that submitter rights may apply. The repository’s MIT license covers its own code. We distribute only source links, public assay annotation, original code and aggregates. **Reopening criterion.** A full specificity test needs public, joinable evidence for the lesion and negative biopsy findings, a comparable anatomical sampling rule or mapped subsites in both groups, and donor/run support after applying those definitions. A paired distant sample would help separate a local field from broader case-peritoneum effects. If such a cohort becomes available, a lesion-specific interpretation would require elevation against both negative groups with quantified uncertainty. Finding a similar shift in ordinary peritoneum or in a distant case baseline would weaken that interpretation. The current evidence supports prioritizing the smaller paired noncase test over another lesion–uterus replication. [Frozen follow-up](NORMAL_PERITONEUM_UTERUS_PLAN_2026-11-16.md)
